March entry. Month: 3. Title: March Bonsai Checklist. Intro: March is when the bonsai collection begins transitioning from winter dormancy toward active growth. Depending on where you live, the timing can vary considerably from year to year, so bud development and weather conditions are more useful guides than the calendar.

Section "Repotting", items: Start checking deciduous trees regularly for signs that the buds are beginning to swell. / For most deciduous species, repotting is best done when the tree is waking up but before the buds have opened and significant new growth begins. / Most conifers can be repotted as their buds swell and begin to move. Check the timing for each species. / Don't feel that you need to repot simply because it's March. A late winter or early spring can shift the timing by several weeks. / Avoid repotting during a period of severe or prolonged cold, particularly when the newly disturbed roots will be exposed to freezing conditions. / Keep records of every repot, including the date, soil mix, root work and container. Use them as a reference, but let the condition of the roots and the tree's vigour decide when it needs attention. / Younger, vigorous trees generally require repotting more frequently than older, established bonsai.

Section "Root and Branch Pruning", items: Root pruning can be carried out as part of spring repotting when the tree is at the appropriate stage. / Preserve as much healthy fine-root structure as possible, particularly on recently collected or weaker trees. / Early spring can also be an excellent time for structural pruning while deciduous trees are still bare. / Avoid combining severe root reduction with heavy top pruning unless the tree is strong enough to tolerate both.

Section "Wiring and Shaping", items: Bare deciduous trees are particularly easy to assess and wire because the branch structure is fully visible. / Early spring can be a good time for structural wiring and shaping on many species. / Check existing wire on trees that are beginning to grow and remove or adjust anything that is starting to bite into the bark.

Section "Coming Out of Winter Protection", items: Don't remove winter protection just because March has arrived. / Many regions experience significant temperature swings during March, including sudden hard freezes after warm spells. / Gradually expose sheltered trees to outdoor conditions as the weather becomes more consistently suitable. / Pay particular attention to trees that have started swelling their buds. Newly activated buds are much more vulnerable to freezing than fully dormant trees. / Keep an eye on overnight temperatures and be prepared to provide additional protection when a hard freeze is forecast.

Section "Fertilizing", items: Wait until a tree has clearly resumed active growth before beginning regular feeding. / Once growth starts, introduce fertilizer gradually rather than immediately applying heavy doses. / Newly repotted trees should be allowed time to recover before receiving strong fertilizer.

Section "Pests and Disease", items: Begin inspecting trees carefully as they emerge from dormancy. / Check for overwintering scale, aphids, mites and other pests, as well as signs of fungal disease. / Early-season or dormant treatments can be useful for certain pests and diseases, but only use products that are appropriate for the species and follow the product label. / Choose a dry, relatively mild day for spraying and avoid applying treatments when freezing temperatures are imminent.

Section "Collecting and Lifting", items: Early spring can be an excellent collection period for many deciduous species, provided the timing is right. / Aim to collect before significant bud break and leaf development. / Preserve as much fine, fibrous root as possible when lifting. / Some evergreens have a longer collection window, but the ideal timing varies by species. / Before collecting anything from the wild or public land, confirm that you have the necessary permission and that collection is legally permitted.

Section "Seeds and Propagation", items: Deciduous seeds that have completed their required cold stratification can be sown. / Check the requirements of each species rather than assuming they all need the same stratification period. / If seeds have not yet been stratified, check the specific species requirements to determine whether there is still enough time to complete the process before spring sowing.

Focus: Don't let the calendar dictate your timing. Watch the trees. When buds begin to swell and the weather starts to stabilize, begin the transition into spring work. Repot, prune and wire when the individual tree is ready, while continuing to protect the collection from unpredictable late-winter freezes.
