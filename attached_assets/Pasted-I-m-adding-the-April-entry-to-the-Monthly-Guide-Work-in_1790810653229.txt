Section "Repotting", items: Early April can still be a good repotting window for trees that haven't been done yet. / Use bud development and the tree's stage of growth as your guide rather than a fixed calendar date. / As deciduous trees begin opening their leaves, the ideal repotting window is generally closing. / Conifers are generally repotted on a similar schedule, as their buds swell and begin to move. Timing varies by species. / Avoid repotting trees that are already pushing substantial new growth unless there is a specific reason to do so. / After repotting, protect trees from unexpected cold snaps and strong winds while the roots recover.

Section "Fertilizing", items: Trees that were not recently repotted can begin receiving regular fertilizer once active growth is underway. / Start with a relatively mild application and increase gradually as growth develops. / Newly repotted trees should not be fertilized immediately. Wait until they have recovered and are showing healthy new growth. / Avoid pushing recently repotted or stressed trees with heavy feeding. / Trees being grown for strong development may benefit from higher nitrogen early in the growing season, while more refined bonsai can be managed with a more balanced approach. / Hold off on feeding flowering trees until flowering has finished and the tree has had time to recover.

Section "Monitoring Spring Growth", items: By April, most trees should be showing obvious signs of renewed activity, although the exact timing varies by species and year. / Watch for unusually early growth, since it is most at risk if a frost is forecast. / Protect newly emerging foliage when significant freezes are forecast. / Check junipers for signs of cedar-apple rust in damp spring weather, especially if apple or crabapple trees are nearby. / Increase watering gradually as temperatures rise and trees begin using more moisture.

Section "Grooming and Development", items: Begin grooming trees that may be displayed later in the season. / Remove unwanted shoots, clean up dead growth and begin managing new growth as it develops. / Don't rush refinement work on trees that are still being developed. Strong spring growth can be valuable for building trunks and branches. / Take advantage of the clear structure of deciduous trees early in the season before the canopy becomes dense.

Section "Seeds and Propagation", items: Continue sowing seeds once they have completed their required stratification. / April can be an excellent time to begin propagating many plants from cuttings. / Evergreen cuttings can often be taken from suitable previous-season growth. / When thinning or trimming suitable conifers and evergreens, save healthy cuttings rather than discarding useful material. / Use clean, healthy shoots with appropriate mature wood and prepare them promptly.

Section "Air-Layering", items: Spring is a good time to begin air-layering suitable trees once they are actively growing. / Junipers, pines, Cryptomeria and other species can sometimes be air-layered successfully, but timing and technique vary by species. / Choose healthy branches with enough diameter and vigour to support the process. / Keep air layers consistently moist once established and monitor them throughout the growing season.

Section "Shelter and Growing Area Cleanup", items: Give your greenhouse or winter shelter and the growing area a thorough spring cleanup before the season gets busy. / Remove old leaves, weeds and accumulated debris from benches and floors. / Clean and disinfect greenhouse surfaces, benches and tools where appropriate. / Pay particular attention to areas underneath benches where pests and fungal pathogens can overwinter. / If wooden benches or staging need treatment, use a product that is safe for an area where plants will be kept and allow adequate curing time before returning trees.

Focus: Spring is underway, but don't assume every tree is on the same schedule. Finish repotting while individual trees are still at the right stage, begin feeding established trees as growth starts, take advantage of the propagation season, and stay alert for late frosts.
