November entry. Month: 11. Title: November Bonsai Checklist. Intro: November is the month when most deciduous trees are settling into full dormancy. Leaves are falling or already gone, while evergreens remain biologically active but at a much slower pace. The focus now is protecting the collection without interrupting the natural dormancy cycle.

Section "Winter Protection", items: November is a good time to have your winter protection fully operational. / Use a cold greenhouse, sheltered area or other appropriate winter setup depending on the species. / The objective is to protect the roots and branches from prolonged extreme cold while still allowing trees that require dormancy to remain cold. / Pines and junipers need a cold winter rest. Keep them outdoors, or in an unheated garage or shed with some light, and never indoors. / Bury the pot in mulch or use a windbreak, and keep junipers out of harsh winter sun. / Watch for sudden temperature drops, strong winds and prolonged freezing periods. / Don't assume that a greenhouse will always remain warmer than the outdoor environment. On very cold nights, additional insulation may be necessary.

Section "Deciduous Trees", items: Most deciduous bonsai should be fully or nearly fully bare by now. / Once the leaves have fallen, take advantage of the clear structure to inspect branches and identify any dead, damaged or unwanted growth. / Don't rush dormant trees into warm conditions. They still need their period of cold dormancy. / However, don't wait for an arbitrary date to provide protection. Move trees into winter storage once the leaves have dropped and there have been several hard frosts, or earlier if severe weather makes it necessary.

Section "Evergreens and Conifers", items: Evergreens continue to function through the winter, although growth and water consumption slow considerably. / Continue checking soil moisture rather than following a fixed watering schedule. / Protect trees from drying winds and extreme temperature swings. / Small bonsai containers require particular attention because their roots are much more exposed to freezing than trees planted in the ground. / Late fall to early spring is the best window for structural work on junipers. Wire carefully, and remove no more than about a third of the foliage at once.

Section "Watering", items: Check sheltered trees regularly for moisture. / Water only when the soil is beginning to dry and conditions are suitable for watering. / Avoid keeping dormant trees constantly wet. / Trees under cover may need more attention than those exposed to natural precipitation.

Section "Finishing Fall Cleanup", items: Leaves keep falling through November, so remove any that land on benches, pots and growing areas. / Remove diseased foliage and branches from the collection and dispose of them away from your growing area. / Depending on local rules, that may mean sealed bags in household garbage, a municipal yard waste program that accepts diseased material, or burning where it is permitted. Check what is allowed where you live, since open burning is restricted in many areas. / Don't add clearly diseased plant material to a home compost pile, where pathogens may survive. / If you suspect a regulated or reportable pest, follow guidance from your local authority.

Section "Planning and Observation", items: November is a good time to review the year's growth and make notes for next season. / Photograph trees while deciduous branches are bare so you can evaluate their structure over winter. / Add a note for each tree alongside its photo, so you can compare next year. / Make a list of trees that may need repotting, pruning, wiring or other work in spring. / Check pots, benches, labels and winter-storage equipment while everything is still accessible.

Focus: Let the trees settle into dormancy, keep them appropriately protected without keeping them warm, monitor moisture, clean up diseased material, and use the bare structure to plan your spring work.
