August entry. Month: 8. Title: August Bonsai Checklist. Intro: August can bring some of the hottest and driest conditions of the growing season. The priority is keeping trees adequately hydrated while managing heat, strong sun and the rapid growth that can cause wire to bite into branches.

Section "Watering and Heat", items: Check trees frequently during hot weather. Bonsai containers can dry out surprisingly quickly. / Water thoroughly when needed rather than relying on a fixed schedule. / During periods of extreme heat, trees in small pots or exposed locations may need attention more than once a day. / Water early in the day whenever possible, and check again later if conditions are exceptionally hot or windy. / Provide temporary shade for species that are prone to leaf scorch or heat stress. / Don't assume that rain has provided enough moisture. Heavy summer showers can wet the surface without thoroughly soaking the root ball.

Section "Fertilizing", items: As summer progresses, gradually reduce the emphasis on high-nitrogen fertilizer, and plan to stop nitrogen by mid-August. / Trees that are still being developed may continue to need feeding, but excessive nitrogen late in the season can encourage soft, vigorous growth that has less time to mature before winter. / A balanced fertilizer or one with relatively less nitrogen can be useful as trees transition toward late-season growth. / Trees being grown for flowers and fruit may benefit from an appropriate phosphorus- and potassium-containing fertilizer, but avoid assuming that more is always better. / Adjust feeding according to the species, tree health and whether your goal is development, refinement, flowering or fruit production.

Section "Wiring", items: Check every wired tree carefully. / August growth can cause branches and trunks to thicken quickly, particularly on vigorous trees. / Loosen or remove wire as soon as it begins to leave noticeable marks. / Don't assume that wire that was safely applied earlier in the season is still safe now. / Keep an eye on rapidly developing branches every few days during periods of strong growth.

Section "Pests and Disease", items: Continue checking foliage, shoots and branches regularly for insects. / Treat pest problems when they appear rather than automatically spraying on a fixed schedule. / Pay particular attention to the undersides of leaves and tender new growth. / Use only treatments appropriate for the species and follow the product label.

Section "Thinning and Needle Work", items: Early August is a good time to thin dense foliage pads on junipers, after summer pinching, so light and air reach the interior and the inner foliage doesn't die back. / In late August, once the new needles on two-flush pines have hardened, pull the oldest, browning needles, usually two or more years old, to let light and air into the inner buds. / Thin gradually, and don't strip weak branches all at once.

Section "Tropical Trees", items: In colder regions, plan to bring tropical trees indoors in late August, before nights drop below about 12°C. / Move them gradually to a bright spot or under grow lights for about 12 to 14 hours a day, since sudden moves can cause leaf drop. / Check for spider mites and scale before bringing trees inside.

Section "Air-Layering", items: Check air layers regularly for developing roots. / Once a layer has produced a strong, well-developed root system, it can eventually be separated and potted. / Don't rush the process. Give the new roots enough time to develop before removing the layer from the parent tree. / After separation, protect newly rooted layers from excessive heat, drying winds and direct sun while they establish.

Section "Vacation Planning", items: Never underestimate how quickly a bonsai collection can dry out during a summer heat wave. / If you're going away, arrange for someone reliable to check and water the trees according to their actual needs. / Make sure the person understands that bonsai may require significantly more attention than ordinary garden plants. / Before leaving, group trees according to their watering requirements and provide shade where appropriate. / An automatic irrigation system can provide additional insurance, but it should be tested thoroughly before relying on it.

Focus: Stay on top of watering, protect trees from excessive heat, monitor pests and wire closely, thin foliage and needles as the calendar calls for, and begin shifting from soft, nitrogen-driven growth toward mature late-season growth.
