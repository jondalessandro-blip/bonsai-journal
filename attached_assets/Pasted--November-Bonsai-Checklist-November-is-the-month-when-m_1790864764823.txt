### November Bonsai Checklist

November is the month when most deciduous trees are settling into full dormancy. Leaves are falling or already gone, while evergreens remain biologically active but at a much slower pace. The focus now is protecting the collection without interrupting the natural dormancy cycle.

**Deciduous Trees**
- Most deciduous bonsai should be fully or nearly fully bare by now.
- Once the leaves have fallen, take advantage of the clear structure to inspect branches and identify any dead, damaged or unwanted growth.
- Don't rush dormant trees into warm conditions. They still need their period of cold dormancy.
- However, don't wait for an arbitrary date to provide protection. Move trees into winter shelter when sustained cold or severe weather makes it necessary.

**Evergreens & Conifers**
- Evergreens continue to function through the winter, although growth and water consumption slow considerably.
- Continue checking soil moisture rather than following a fixed watering schedule.
- Protect trees from drying winds and extreme temperature swings.
- Small bonsai containers require particular attention because their roots are much more exposed to freezing than trees planted in the ground.

**Watering**
- Check sheltered trees regularly for moisture.
- Water only when the soil is beginning to dry and conditions are suitable for watering.
- Avoid keeping dormant trees constantly wet.
- Trees under cover may need more attention than those exposed to natural precipitation.

**Winter Protection**
- November is a good time to have your winter protection fully operational.
- Use a cold greenhouse, sheltered area or other appropriate winter setup depending on the species.
- The objective is to protect the roots and branches from prolonged extreme cold while still allowing trees that require dormancy to remain cold.
- Watch for sudden temperature drops, strong winds and prolonged freezing periods.
- Don't assume that a greenhouse will always remain warmer than the outdoor environment; on very cold nights, additional insulation may be necessary.

**Fall Cleanup**
- Remove fallen leaves and other debris from benches, pots and growing areas.
- Diseased foliage and branches should be removed from the collection and disposed of appropriately.
- Avoid burning material unless local regulations and conditions permit it.
- Don't add clearly diseased plant material to compost where the pathogens may survive.
- Clean tools and work surfaces before winter.

**Planning & Observation**
- November is a good time to review the year's growth and make notes for next season.
- Photograph trees while deciduous branches are bare so you can evaluate their structure over winter.
- Make a list of trees that may need repotting, pruning, wiring or other work in spring.
- Check pots, benches, labels and winter-storage equipment while everything is still accessible.

**November focus:** Let the trees settle into dormancy, keep them appropriately protected without keeping them warm, monitor moisture, clean up diseased material, and use the bare structure to plan your spring work.
