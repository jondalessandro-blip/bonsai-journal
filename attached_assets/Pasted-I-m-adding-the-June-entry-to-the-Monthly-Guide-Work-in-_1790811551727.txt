June entry. Month: 6. Title: June Bonsai Checklist. Intro: Summer starts now. In northern temperate climates, June brings the longest days of the year and the first real summer heat. Growth is at its peak.

Section "Watering and Heat", items: Watering is the priority in June. Check your trees at least once a day and water whenever the surface of the soil begins to dry. / Evening is a good time to water. In hot, dry spells, water twice: once early in the morning before the sun hits the foliage, and again in the evening. / If it has rained hard during the day, check before watering, since the pots may already be well soaked. / Avoid watering in the heat of the midday sun.

Section "Fertilizing", items: Growth is at its peak, so this is a good time to feed established trees regularly. / One simple rhythm is a slow-release organic granular feed at the start of the month, with a weak, high-potash liquid feed added once a week in between. / You can also use either one on its own: an organic granular feed replaced about monthly, or a diluted liquid feed every one to two weeks. / Don't feed weak, dry or freshly repotted trees. / Plan to stop nitrogen by mid-August so new growth hardens before winter.

Section "Pinching and Pruning", items: Trees are pushing hard this month, so keep pinching new shoots to hold their shape. / On deciduous trees, let new shoots grow out to about five or six leaves, then cut back to about two to build fine, twiggy branching. Repeat through early summer. / Go lightly on crabapples and other flowering trees. They set next year's flower buds in summer, and heavy pinching removes them. / On single-flush pines, pinch or snap off about a third to a half of each strong candle before the needles open. Leave weak candles alone, and never remove the whole candle. / On two-flush pines, candle cutting usually comes in mid to late June, once the candles are fully stretched and the needles are just starting to separate. Cut strong candles back by about a third and medium ones by about half, and leave weak candles alone. Do this on strong, healthy trees only, and check the timing for your species. / On junipers, pinch new tips with your fingers to keep the foliage compact. Never strip a branch bare, since junipers rarely regrow from bare wood.

Section "Pests and Disease", items: Aphids, both green and black, increase quickly now. Check the undersides of leaves regularly and treat at the first sign. / Keep checking junipers for cedar-apple rust in damp weather, especially if apple or crabapple trees are nearby.

Section "Propagation", items: Keep doing air-layers on both evergreen and deciduous trees. / Early June is a good time for softwood cuttings. Chinese elm, zelkova and trident maple commonly root within a few weeks in good conditions. Japanese maple is much harder to root from cuttings. / Seedlings started in March should have two to three pairs of leaves by now and can be separated into their own pots.

Section "Wiring", items: Wiring can be done any time of year, including June. / Be gentle to avoid damaging the soft new leaves on deciduous trees.

Section "Leaf Pruning (Advanced, Optional)", items: From early June to mid-July, strong deciduous trees can be defoliated or partially leaf-pruned to encourage a second flush of smaller leaves. This is commonly done with maple, beech and sycamore. / This is for experienced growers and strong, healthy, well-fed trees only. Never do it on weak or freshly repotted trees. / On Japanese maple, remove leaves only partially. / Don't do it after mid-July, and skip it entirely where the growing season is short.

Section "Tropical Trees", items: Move tropical trees outdoors once nights stay reliably warm. Start them in shade and move them to more sun over about a week, since direct sun straight after a winter indoors burns leaves. / Feed regularly while they are actively growing outdoors. / Summer is the time to prune hard and build structure on tropicals. They are also repotted in the warm months when growth is fastest, unlike hardy trees. / Try not to hard prune and repot the same tree in the same week.

Focus: Growth is at its peak and summer heat has arrived. Water carefully, feed steadily, and shape new growth as it comes, while staying alert for pests.
