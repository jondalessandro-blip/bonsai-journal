Section "Watering and Sun", items: As growth speeds up, trees use much more water. Check the soil every day and water whenever it starts to dry. / Water thoroughly so it runs through the drainage holes, and avoid a light sprinkle that only wets the surface. / Watch trees under cover or in a greenhouse closely during warm spells, since they can dry out quickly. / Make sure any greenhouse vents and shade cloth work properly so sunny days don't overheat the space.

Section "Late Frost Protection", items: New leaves are tender and can be damaged by a late frost, and the marks can stay on the leaves for the rest of the season. / Keep an eye on the forecast and be ready to move tender trees under cover on cold nights. / Trees with very early or soft growth, such as many maples, are the most vulnerable. / Bring tropical trees outdoors only once nights stay reliably warm.

Section "Pinching and Shoot Care", items: Pinch back long, vigorous shoots on deciduous trees with your fingers as they extend, to keep the branch structure compact. / Pinch the candles on single-flush pines once they have stretched but before the needles open. / Pinch the soft growth tips on junipers as new shoots reach a few centimetres. / Let trees you are building up grow more freely, and save refinement for trees that are already developed.

Section "Fertilizing", items: Keep feeding established trees. Start deciduous trees once their first leaves have firmed up. / Begin junipers as growth starts, and feed pines as their candles grow. / Hold off on strong fertilizer for trees that were repotted recently, until they show healthy new growth.

Section "Seeds and Cuttings", items: Sowing is getting late in many regions, since seeds sown later in the season have less time to establish before winter. / Cuttings from many species can still be taken. Use clean, healthy shoots and keep them evenly moist.

Section "Air-Layering", items: Air-layering deciduous trees can begin once they are in active growth and continue through summer. / Continue with evergreen layers already started, and keep every layer consistently moist.

Section "Pests and Disease", items: Check new growth regularly for aphids, caterpillars and other early pests. / Check junipers for cedar-apple rust in damp weather, especially if apple or crabapple trees are nearby.

Focus: Growth is fast and the weather is still unsettled. Water consistently, guard tender new leaves against a late frost, and pinch and feed as each tree's growth calls for it.
