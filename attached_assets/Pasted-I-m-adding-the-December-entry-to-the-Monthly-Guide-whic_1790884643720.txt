December entry. Month: 12. Title: December Bonsai Checklist. Intro: By December, most hardy bonsai should be fully dormant and winter protection should be in place. The goal isn't to keep trees warm, but to protect their roots and branches from prolonged extreme cold while allowing them to remain properly dormant.

Section "Winter Protection", items: Make sure your winter protection is fully operational before the coldest weather arrives. / Less hardy trees, such as Japanese and trident maples, are best moved into a cold greenhouse or other suitable winter shelter once sustained freezing makes extra protection necessary. / In milder regions, December is when winter storage happens. Once the leaves have dropped and there have been several hard frosts, move trees into an unheated shelter, garage or shed, never into a heated house. / A simple sheltered structure can work well. A sturdy frame covered with greenhouse plastic can provide protection from wind, snow and extreme temperature swings. / Keep protected trees cold but not warm. Avoid heated environments that could cause them to break dormancy prematurely. / Pines, junipers, cedars and other hardy conifers still need cold. Keep them outdoors or in an unheated shelter with some light, never in a warm house. / Broadleaf evergreens vary a lot in hardiness. Check your species: hardy types can overwinter like conifers, while less hardy ones need a cool, bright, frost-free spot. They keep losing water through winter, so check their moisture regularly. / Pay particular attention to bonsai in small containers. Their roots can be exposed to much lower temperatures than those of trees growing in the ground. / During periods of extreme cold, additional insulation around pots or at ground level may be necessary.

Section "Watering", items: Dormant trees still require occasional moisture. / Check the soil periodically, particularly for trees kept under cover where they receive no natural precipitation. / As a rule of thumb, check stored pots every two to three weeks. Evergreens keep losing water through winter and need this most. / Water when the soil is beginning to dry and conditions are suitable. / Avoid keeping dormant trees saturated, but don't allow the root ball to become completely dry. / On extremely cold days, leave the trees undisturbed and water when temperatures moderate.

Section "Shelter Management", items: Ventilate the greenhouse or shelter on sunny days if temperatures rise significantly. / Check for excessive condensation and poor air circulation. / Make sure drainage remains clear and pots aren't sitting in standing water. / Inspect the structure after snow, ice or strong winds to ensure it remains secure.

Section "Winter Monitoring", items: Check trees periodically for damage from rodents, snow, ice or wind. / Make sure labels remain secure and readable. / Keep an eye on weather forecasts so additional protection can be added before an extreme cold event rather than afterward.

Section "Pests and Disease", items: Inspect trees periodically for overwintering pests and signs of disease. / A dormant-season treatment may be appropriate for certain species and pest problems. / Don't spray simply because it's December. Use treatments when there is a specific reason and when weather conditions are suitable. / If treatment is necessary, choose a dry, relatively mild day and follow the product label carefully. / Avoid spraying when freezing temperatures are imminent or when the tree is already exposed to severe cold.

Section "Winter Styling (Advanced, Optional)", items: Late fall to early spring is the best window for structural work on junipers. Wire carefully, and remove no more than about a third of the foliage at once. / On single-flush pines, winter is a good time for bud selection, when the buds are easy to see. Where several buds sit at one point, keep about two pointing sideways and remove the extras to avoid ugly whorls. / This is optional work for experienced growers.

Section "Tropical Trees", items: Tropical trees should be indoors under light by now. / Maintenance only: water when the soil is slightly dry, and for succulents only when fully dry. / Light pinching is fine, but no heavy work.

Section "Planning Ahead", items: December is a good time to review your repotting records and begin planning spring work. / Identify trees that may need repotting, major pruning, wiring or other attention. / Order soil components, pots, wire and other supplies before the spring rush. / Use the dormant season to study the structure of deciduous trees and plan your next steps.

Focus: Keep hardy trees cold and dormant while protecting them from winter extremes. Monitor moisture, pests and the shelter environment, and use the quiet season to prepare for spring.
